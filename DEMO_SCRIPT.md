# FounderScore · 2-minute demo

## Setup before recording

- Keep one completed rich inbound application open in a browser tab.
- Keep the Pipeline and Thesis pages ready in separate tabs; do not wait for a live deck to process during the recording.
- At 90% browser zoom, show sources by clicking one citation and then return to the memo.
- Do not show terminal windows, API keys, or a raw long memo.

## Narration and clicks

**0:00–0:10 — problem**

> Capital still flows through who a founder knows. FounderScore turns an application and scattered public work into an evidence-backed decision path within 24 hours.

Open **Pipeline**.

> This is one funnel: inbound applications and founders surfaced through GitHub, launches, hackathons, papers, and accelerator signals are deduplicated into Memory before review.

Point to **Sourcing intelligence**.

> This graph is a clearly labeled prototype of sourcing and network intelligence. It shows the channels through which founders become visible and a next scan; production learns from conversion outcomes, not volume alone.

**0:10–0:28 — thesis and multi-attribute reasoning**

Open **Thesis** briefly, then return to Pipeline. Type `technical founder, Berlin, AI infra, no prior VC backing` and press Enter.

> The investor configures sector, stage, geography, check size, ownership, and risk. This is one compound natural-language query, resolved in one pass—not five manual filters.

**0:28–0:45 — inclusive inbound**

Open **Apply**.

> A deck and company name are the minimum. Optional exact public links and cofounder profiles sharpen confidence, but a founder does not need a warm introduction. We ingest documents, slides, embedded images, public company evidence, and supplied profiles into a timestamped source ledger.

**0:45–1:18 — decision-ready opportunity**

Open a completed opportunity.

> The Founder Score belongs to Memory and persists across evidence updates. It is one input—not a replacement—for three independent axes: Founder, Market, and Idea versus Market. We never average those axes because disagreement is the decision signal.

Point to founder cards, affiliation chips, and source icons.

> Exact supplied public profiles can yield a verified portrait plus concise affiliations such as prior roles and school. If the profile provider cannot make an exact match, we show an intentional fallback, never a guessed identity.

Click a deck/source citation.

> Every conclusion has a source trail—deck slide, supplied profile, GitHub, or bounded public reference. The Trust Score is per claim, not one confidence score for the company.

**1:18–1:48 — diligence and memo**

Show the processing trace, trust section, market section, and short memo.

> The Validator cross-references claims and flags contradictions before they reach the investor. The memo stays brief: company snapshot, hypotheses, SWOT, problem and product, and traction. Missing cap table, financials, or customer evidence are flagged—not fabricated. The adversarial view explains what could break the strongest case.

**1:48–2:00 — close**

Point to the recommendation/decision control.

> FounderScore moves from **Sourcing → Screening → Diligence → Decision** with confidence, uncertainty, and evidence visible at every step. It recommends the next decision; a human partner retains final oversight.

## Phrases to use exactly

- “Three independent axes—never averaged.”
- “Per-claim Trust Score.”
- “Memory retains timestamped, deduplicated evidence.”
- “Cold-start founders can apply without a prior network.”
- “Bounded public evidence, not unbounded scraping.”
- “Human oversight at the final decision.”
